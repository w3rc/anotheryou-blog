<h2>"How long can the insurer take at discharge?"</h2>
<p>Three hours. Under IRDAI's master circular on health insurance (May 2024), an insurer has to decide a cashless request within one hour of the hospital asking, and give the final authorisation at discharge within three hours of the hospital's request. If it takes longer and the hospital charges extra for the delay, the insurer has to bear that amount, not you.</p>
<p>And for a cashless claim, the insurer or TPA collects the papers from the hospital. You shouldn't be sent running for documents the hospital already has.</p>

<h2>"Why did they cut the claim?"</h2>
<p>Ask for it in writing, with the exact clause. "As per policy terms" isn't an answer. The usual reasons are:</p>
<ul class="tight">
<li>A room rent cap. Take a costlier room than the cap allows (say, 1% of the sum insured a day) and many policies shrink the whole bill in proportion, doctors' fees included.</li>
<li>A sub-limit on that illness or procedure, or a co-payment (common on senior citizen plans).</li>
<li>Items the policy lists as not covered.</li>
</ul>
<p>Some of these are real. Some are applied wrongly. You can only tell which once you have the clause.</p>

<h2>"They rejected it for a pre-existing disease"</h2>
<p>Check how long the cover has run without a break (renewals and porting count). IRDAI's rules from 2024 cap the waiting period for pre-existing diseases at 3 years. And after 60 months of continuous cover, an insurer can't reject a claim for non-disclosure, a pre-existing disease included, except for proven fraud. A "pre-existing disease" rejection after six years of continuous cover is very likely wrong. Collect every year's policy schedule to prove the continuity.</p>

<h2>"Does it end at the grievance cell?"</h2>
<p>No. Here's the route, in order:</p>
<ol class="tight">
<li><strong>The insurer's grievance officer,</strong> in writing, quoting the clause they used and the rule that answers it. Keep the acknowledgement. A reply is due in about 14 days.</li>
<li><strong>Bima Bharosa,</strong> IRDAI's own complaint portal (bimabharosa.irdai.gov.in, toll free 155255). IRDAI forwards it to the insurer and tracks it.</li>
<li><strong>The Insurance Ombudsman</strong> (cioins.co.in). It opens once the insurer rejects your grievance or doesn't reply within 30 days. File within one year of the insurer's final reply. It's free, you don't need a lawyer, and it hears claims up to ₹50 lakh.</li>
<li>Or the consumer commission, on e-Daakhil, especially above ₹50 lakh.</li>
</ol>

<h2>What to keep, starting today</h2>
<p>The policy schedule for every year, the claim forms, the discharge summary, the final bill with its item list, and the insurer's letter. One folder. It's your whole case.</p>

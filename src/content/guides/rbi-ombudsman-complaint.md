<h3>Why are we publishing this?</h3>
<p>Because a lot of advice online still says you have a year to complain to the RBI Ombudsman. Since 1 July 2026 you have 90 days. Miss them and your complaint can be rejected without anyone reading it.</p>
<p>The Ombudsman is free, you don't need a lawyer, and RBI itself says you don't need to pay anyone to file for you. Here's how it works, from RBI's own rules.</p>
</div>

<h2>Step 1: the bank has to hear it first, in writing</h2>
<p>RBI won't take your complaint unless you complained to the bank (or NBFC, loan app lender or wallet) first, and can prove it. A phone call isn't proof. Use the bank's grievance page or email, or hand in a letter at the branch and get a stamped copy. Keep the complaint number and the date.</p>
<p>Then the bank gets 30 days. You can go to the Ombudsman when:</p>
<ul>
<li>30 days pass with no reply (or longer, if an NPCI or card network rule gives the bank more time), or</li>
<li>the bank replies before that and you're not happy with what it says.</li>
</ul>
<p>File too early and it's rejected.</p>

<h2>Step 2: the 90 day window (the old one year is gone)</h2>
<p>You have 90 days to file, counted from whichever comes later: the end of the bank's 30 days, or the bank's last reply to you.</p>
<p>An example. Your bank gets your complaint on 1 December 2026 and never replies. Its 30 days run out on 31 December. You can file from then, and your 90 days end on 31 March 2027.</p>
<p>One more limit sits behind this: your complaint to the bank itself has to be made within the normal legal time limit for that kind of claim. Don't sit on an old problem.</p>

<h2>Step 3: file it at cms.rbi.org.in</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Online:</strong> cms.rbi.org.in. The form is in Hindi and English, but you can paste your story in any language.</li>
<li><strong>Email:</strong> crpc@rbi.org.in, in any language.</li>
<li><strong>Post:</strong> Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre, Reserve Bank of India, Central Vista, Sector 17, Chandigarh 160017, signed, with your documents.</li>
</ul>
<p>Helpline 14448 can tell you your complaint's status and how to file, but it can't take a complaint. Have these ready: your name, mobile, email and address; the bank's name and branch; your complaint number to the bank with its date and a copy; the account, card, loan or transaction details; the bank's reply if there was one; what happened, what it cost you, and what you want done.</p>
<p>You can file through someone you authorise in writing, but not through a lawyer (unless the lawyer is the one with the complaint).</p>

<h2>What it can make the bank do</h2>
<p>There's no limit on the amount in dispute. Most complaints end in a settlement the Ombudsman helps you and the bank reach. If there's no settlement and it finds the bank fell short, it can pass an Award ordering the bank to fix the problem and pay you: up to ₹30 lakh for loss you suffered because of it, plus up to ₹3 lakh for your time, expenses, harassment and mental anguish.</p>
<p>If you get an Award, send the bank a letter accepting it within 30 days, and the bank then has 30 days to comply. Unhappy with an Award? You can appeal within 30 days. There's no appeal if your complaint was simply rejected.</p>

<h2>What it won't take</h2>
<ul>
<li>Anything you didn't complain to the bank about first.</li>
<li>A complaint filed before the 30 days, or after the 90.</li>
<li>The same problem already in a court, consumer commission or arbitration. A police case is different and doesn't stop you.</li>
<li>The bank's business decisions, like refusing you a loan.</li>
<li>Lenders outside the scheme. It covers banks, NBFCs that take deposits or deal with customers and have assets of ₹100 crore or more, wallet companies and the credit bureaus. Housing finance companies aren't covered, and an unregistered loan app isn't an RBI lender at all.</li>
</ul>

<h2>5 rules that make the bank pay you for every day it's late</h2>
<p>Some RBI rules come with a daily penalty paid to you. Quote them in your complaint.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Failed UPI or IMPS transfer.</strong> Money left your account but never reached the other person. It must come back by the next day (T+1), or the bank pays you ₹100 a day after that. For an ATM that didn't give cash, or a card or UPI payment to a shop that didn't go through, the limit is 5 days, then ₹100 a day. The bank is supposed to pay this without you asking.</li>
<li><strong>Credit card you asked to close.</strong> The card company has 7 working days once you've no dues left. After that it owes you ₹500 for every day it's late.</li>
<li><strong>Property papers after you repay a loan.</strong> The bank has 30 days after full repayment or settlement to return your original documents and remove its charge. If the delay is on its side, it pays you ₹5,000 for each day.</li>
<li><strong>A mistake on your credit report.</strong> If your complaint to the lender or the credit bureau (CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, CRIF) isn't resolved in 30 calendar days, you get ₹100 for every day after that.</li>
<li><strong>A card you never asked for.</strong> If a card or upgrade was issued without your consent and you were billed, the charges have to be reversed and you get a penalty of twice the charges.</li>
</ol>

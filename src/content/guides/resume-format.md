<h3>Why are we publishing this?</h3>
<p>Search "resume format" and you get templates: two columns, a photo in the corner, skill bars, icons. They look great. Then search what an actual employer asks for, and it's much plainer.</p>
<p>Google's careers team publishes a short resume guide for candidates. Below are its rules, in plain words, and how they apply to a resume for a job in India.</p>
</div>

<h2>The 7 rules</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Send a PDF.</strong> It's the first thing on Google's list.</li>
<li><strong>One page if you have under 10 years of relevant experience. Two pages at most.</strong> Not three, whatever your seniority.</li>
<li><strong>Put the right section first.</strong> Students and freshers lead with education, because it's their most recent thing. Working people lead with work experience and move education to the end.</li>
<li><strong>Write each bullet as a result.</strong> Google's formula: "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]". So not "Responsible for social media", but "Grew Instagram followers from 4,000 to 22,000 in 9 months by posting 4 reels a week". Google suggests about half your detail explains the role and half says what you personally achieved.</li>
<li><strong>No objective.</strong> If you want a summary, keep it to 1 or 2 sentences, and use it to show how you fit the job's minimum and preferred qualifications.</li>
<li><strong>No references section, and no home address.</strong> The recruiter asks for references if they need them. Email is the standard way to reach you.</li>
<li><strong>A gap is your call.</strong> You don't have to explain it. If you do, a line like "2023 to 2025: Time off spent with family" is enough. Add any course or side project from that time, and be ready to talk about it if asked.</li>
</ol>
<p>And one rule over all of them: read the job's qualifications before you apply, and make sure your achievements show them.</p>

<h2>The format for your years of experience</h2>
<h3>Fresher or student (0 to 1 year)</h3>
<p>One page. Order: name and contact, a 1 or 2 line summary (optional), education (college, degree, graduation month and year, grades if you want, relevant coursework and certifications), then internships, projects and work, then activities, awards and skills. Treat projects like jobs: what you built, with what, and what happened.</p>
<h3>1 to 10 years</h3>
<p>Still one page. Order: name and contact, a short summary, work experience (most recent first, each role with the employer, title and dates, then result bullets), skills, then education and certifications at the end. Older roles get fewer bullets.</p>
<h3>10 years or more</h3>
<p>Up to two pages. Same order. Keep the detail for recent roles; roles from long ago can shrink to one line each.</p>

<h2>What isn't on Google's list</h2>
<p>Many Indian resume templates still carry these. None of them appears anywhere in Google's guide. Leave them off unless the employer or the application form asks:</p>
<ul>
<li>A photo</li>
<li>Date of birth, marital status, religion or father's name</li>
<li>Your full home address (your city is plenty)</li>
<li>Current or expected CTC</li>
<li>"Career objective"</li>
<li>"References available on request"</li>
<li>"Declaration: I hereby declare that the above information is true..."</li>
</ul>

<h2>The 30 second check before you send</h2>
<p>Many companies put resumes through software before a person sees them, and that software reads the text inside your file, not the design. So open your PDF, select everything, copy it, and paste it into a plain notes app.</p>
<p>Does it come out in the right order: your name, then your jobs, with each job's dates beside it? Or are two columns jumbled together, with your skills in the middle of a job and your phone number missing because it was an icon or an image? What you see in the notes app is a rough preview of what a machine gets. If it's a mess, move to a single column with plain headings (Experience, Education, Skills) and plain text for your contact details.</p>

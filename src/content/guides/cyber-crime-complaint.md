<h3>Why are we publishing this?</h3>
<p>Because of questions like this one. Someone on Quora lost ₹90,000 from their SBI account to a UPI scammer at 4:16 pm, and asked the next day: what should I do now?</p>
<p>The answer depends on what you do in the first few hours and on dates most people never write down. Nobody can promise you the money back, and anyone who does is lying. But the steps below give you the best chance the rules allow.</p>
</div>

<h2>The first hour, in this order</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Call 1930.</strong> It's the National Cyber Crime Helpline, run by your state police. It's free and you can call any time. The officer files your case on the government's fraud system, which alerts the banks your money passed through and asks them to hold it.</li>
<li><strong>Tell your bank, in writing, the same day.</strong> Use the fraud option in the bank's app, the number in the debit SMS, or the branch, and ask them to block the card, UPI or net banking. Get a complaint number. Write down the date and time you reported. Under RBI's rules, that date can decide who pays (see below).</li>
<li><strong>File on cybercrime.gov.in with your 14 digit number.</strong> After the 1930 call you get an SMS from a sender like "XXNCRP" with a 14 digit acknowledgement number starting with 3. The SMS asks you to add the full details on cybercrime.gov.in using that number. Do it today.</li>
<li><strong>Save the proof.</strong> Every debit SMS and email, the UTR or transaction ID of each payment, screenshots of the chat, the scammer's number, UPI ID, website or app name, and both reference numbers (1930 and bank).</li>
<li><strong>Don't pay anyone who offers to get it back.</strong> 1930, cybercrime.gov.in, your bank, the police and the RBI Ombudsman are all free. A caller offering "fund recovery" for a fee is the second scam, and it often finds victims within days.</li>
</ol>
<p>Paid by UPI? Also raise a dispute inside the UPI app. It goes to the bank behind the app.</p>

<h2>Why #2 can decide whether the bank pays</h2>
<p>RBI's rules on customer liability (now in its Responsible Business Conduct Directions, 2025) depend on two things: how the money left, and how fast you told the bank.</p>
<p><strong>If money left without you doing anything</strong> (a card cloned, a SIM swap, a debit you never made) and the fault was neither yours nor the bank's, the clock counts working days from the bank's alert:</p>
<ul>
<li>Within 3 working days: <strong>zero liability</strong>. You lose nothing.</li>
<li>Within 4 to 7 working days: your loss is capped at the transaction amount or ₹5,000, ₹10,000 or ₹25,000, whichever is lower, depending on your account or card type.</li>
<li>After 7 working days: whatever the bank's own policy says.</li>
</ul>
<p>If the bank itself was at fault, your liability is zero however late you report. Once you've reported, the bank must put the amount back in your account within 10 working days while it checks, and decide the complaint within 90 days. It's on the bank to prove you were at fault, not on you to prove you weren't.</p>
<p><strong>If you shared an OTP, PIN or card details</strong>, or installed an app the scammer sent, the bank treats it as your negligence: you carry the loss until you report it, and the bank carries every rupee lost after that. So report even when you think it's your fault.</p>
<p><strong>If you sent the money yourself</strong> (scanned a QR code "to receive money", paid a fake seller, a job or "task" scam, an investment group), these rules usually don't cover it. Then the 1930 call is what matters: it's the step that can freeze the money in the account it went to.</p>
<p>The rules change for fraud on or after 1 January 2027: the zero liability window becomes 5 calendar days from the fraud, payments made under threat are covered, and some small losses get part compensation. If yours happened before that, the rules above apply.</p>

<h2>"The shadow amount is marked for the ticket." What does that mean?</h2>
<p>Good news, mostly. Your bank has put the disputed amount back into your own account provisionally while it investigates. You can see it, but you can't spend or withdraw it yet. It becomes normal money when the bank decides in your favour. If it decides against you, it can take it back, and it has to give reasons. You don't need to visit the branch unless the bank asks for papers. Ask for the decision date in writing.</p>

<h2>"Amount put on hold": how frozen money comes back</h2>
<p>"Hold" or "lien" means money frozen in the fraudster's account, the one your money went to. It isn't yours yet, and it's often less than you lost, because some had already moved on. Under the Home Ministry's procedure for the fraud system (January 2026):</p>
<ol>
<li>Check your case on cybercrime.gov.in with your acknowledgement number. If an amount is on hold, apply for its return through the portal's Money Restoration Module. The request goes to the police station handling your case.</li>
<li>Below ₹50,000, the procedure doesn't ask for an FIR. Above ₹50,000, the release has to be connected to an FIR, so ask the police station in writing to register one, quoting your acknowledgement number.</li>
<li>The investigating officer gives the account holder notice within 7 days and up to 15 days to explain the money.</li>
<li>If the money is found to be yours, the officer (with the SP's or DCP's approval) tells the bank to pay you, once you sign an indemnity bond: a promise to give it back if a court later says so.</li>
<li>The bank has to pay within 15 calendar days of that order.</li>
</ol>
<p>The other route is the magistrate's court, which can order the release too. Your district's legal services authority can help with that for free.</p>

<h2>When the bank says no: the RBI Ombudsman</h2>
<p>If the bank rejects your complaint, or doesn't reply within 30 days, file with the RBI Ombudsman at cms.rbi.org.in. Since 1 July 2026 you have 90 days to do it, counted from the bank's last reply or the end of that 30 day wait, whichever is later. (The old one year window is gone, and a lot of advice online still quotes it.) It's free, you don't need a lawyer, there's no limit on the amount, and on top of money actually lost it can award up to ₹3 lakh for your time, expenses and harassment. A police case running at the same time doesn't stop you.</p>

<h2>"Is there any actual deadline, or do they just keep moving it?"</h2>
<p>There is one. Under the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020, every shopping site and marketplace has to appoint a grievance officer, publish their name and contact details on the site or app, acknowledge your complaint within 48 hours, and resolve it within one month.</p>
<p>The chatbot isn't the grievance officer. The ticket it closed isn't a complaint to them. That's the first thing to fix.</p>

<h2>"Where do I even find the grievance officer?"</h2>
<p>Scroll to the bottom of the website, or open the app's help or legal section, and look for "Grievance Officer" or "Grievance Redressal". The name and email have to be there. Write to that email, and copy customer care.</p>

<h2>"When is it fair to escalate?"</h2>
<p>Once a return is picked up and checked, refunds to cards and UPI usually take 5 to 7 working days, and the bank can add a few more. Three weeks after pickup with no money is a fair point to stop waiting and write.</p>

<h2>"What should I keep?"</h2>
<ul class="tight">
<li>The order ID and the return request ID.</li>
<li>The pickup confirmation: date, courier, tracking (AWB) number.</li>
<li>Screenshots of the refund status, and every chat or email.</li>
</ul>
<p>If the site says the refund was sent and your bank says nothing arrived, ask the site for the reference number (ARN or RRN) and give it to your bank. That one number ends most "we sent it" arguments.</p>

<h2>The email that gets answered</h2>
<p>Short, dated and calm. Consumer commissions read these later, so write it for them.</p>
<ol class="tight">
<li><strong>Subject:</strong> "Complaint: refund not received, order [number]".</li>
<li>Your name, order number, date and amount.</li>
<li>What happened, in 3 to 6 short dated lines.</li>
<li>The rule: "Under the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020, this complaint must be resolved within one month."</li>
<li>Exactly what you want: "a refund of ₹X to my original payment method".</li>
<li>A deadline: 7 days for a simple refund.</li>
<li>What happens if it's ignored: "I will take this to the National Consumer Helpline and the District Consumer Commission."</li>
<li>The list of what you've attached.</li>
</ol>

<h2>"National Consumer Helpline didn't resolve my complaint"</h2>
<p>The National Consumer Helpline is free. Call 1915, use WhatsApp, or file on the NCH app or consumerhelpline.gov.in. It forwards your docket to the company, and e-commerce firms tend to answer it quickly. Many complaints end here.</p>
<p>If it doesn't end there, it was still worth it: it's one more dated record that you tried.</p>

<h2>"How do I file a consumer complaint in India?"</h2>
<p>Online, on e-Daakhil (edaakhil.nic.in), with no lawyer. Under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019:</p>
<ul class="tight">
<li>Claims up to ₹50 lakh go to the District Commission.</li>
<li>There's no fee for claims up to ₹5 lakh.</li>
<li>You can file where you live or work.</li>
<li>You have 2 years from the problem.</li>
</ul>

<h2>"Can I get compensation, or just my money back?"</h2>
<p>Both. A consumer commission can order the refund, compensation for the harassment, and your costs. That's why the paper trail above matters: it's the harassment, in dates.</p>

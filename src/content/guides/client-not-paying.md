<p>Because this is the freelancer question we kept finding, asked again and again on Quora:</p>
<blockquote>"My client is not paying me for my freelance work. What should I do?"</blockquote>
<p>Most of the answer was decided earlier: by three lines in a contract signed in an hour, and by one free registration most freelancers never do. Here's both. No sign up to read it, nothing to buy.</p>

<h2>"My client is not paying me. What should I do?"</h2>
<p>Start with a written reminder (email, not a call) that attaches the invoice, says the date it was due, and gives a new date: 7 days is fair. If your contract lets you pause work on an overdue invoice, say you're pausing. Polite, dated and boring wins here. A client who later claims "we never got it" has an email thread saying otherwise.</p>
<p>What happens after that depends on two things: whether your client is in India, and whether you were registered as a micro enterprise before you took the job.</p>

<h2>The free registration that changes everything: Udyam</h2>
<p>Udyam registration is the government's MSME registration. It's free, online (udyamregistration.gov.in), and takes your Aadhaar and PAN. A freelancer selling services can register as a micro enterprise.</p>
<p>Do it before you sign the next contract. The Supreme Court has held that the MSME law's payment protection doesn't reach back to work agreed before you registered, so the registration date matters.</p>

<h2>45 days, then interest at three times the RBI rate</h2>
<p>Once you're registered, the MSMED Act, 2006 says an Indian buyer has to pay you within the agreed period, and that period can't be longer than 45 days from acceptance (15 days if nothing was agreed). That's section 15.</p>
<p>Section 16 is the one clients don't expect. Pay late, and they owe compound interest, with monthly rests, at three times the bank rate notified by RBI. At recent bank rates that's well over 15% a year. And it applies "notwithstanding anything contained in any agreement", so a contract saying "net 90" doesn't switch it off.</p>

<h2>The tax rule your client's accountant cares about</h2>
<p>Since 1 April 2024, section 43B(h) of the Income Tax Act says a business can't claim a payment owed to a micro or small enterprise as an expense in its tax return until it actually pays, if it pays after the 45 day limit. Late payment to you now costs the client tax. Mention it, calmly, in your second reminder. Finance teams move faster for this line than for any number of "just following up" emails.</p>

<h2>"Can I take them to court for ₹50,000?"</h2>
<p>You don't have to start with a court. File on the MSME Samadhaan portal (samadhaan.msme.gov.in). The case goes to your state's Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council, which first tries conciliation and then decides it. And if it rules for you and the client wants to challenge that in court, section 19 makes them deposit 75% of the amount first.</p>
<p>All of this is for Indian clients only, and only if you were registered before the work.</p>

<h2>"What about my foreign clients?"</h2>
<p>The MSME law doesn't reach a client in the US or the UK, and a contract that sends disputes to arbitration in Singapore or London costs more to enforce than most freelance invoices are worth. So with foreign clients, your protection is the contract itself:</p>
<ul class="tight">
<li>Work through the platform's escrow (Upwork, Fiverr) where you can; their dispute process is your court.</li>
<li>Off platform, take an advance of 30% to 50% and bill in milestones, so the most you can ever lose is one milestone.</li>
<li>Ask for Indian law, or at least mediation or online dispute resolution for small amounts, before any foreign arbitration clause.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The 3 lines to add before you sign the next contract</h2>
<ol class="tight">
<li><strong>Advance and milestones:</strong> "30% on signing, the rest in milestones as set out below."</li>
<li><strong>Deemed acceptance:</strong> "Deliverables are accepted unless the Client gives specific written reasons for rejection within 7 days of delivery." Without it, a client can delay "accepting" forever, and nothing becomes due.</li>
<li><strong>Payment and a late fee:</strong> "Invoices are payable within 15 days. Overdue amounts carry interest at 1.5% a month, and the Freelancer may pause work while any invoice is more than 15 days overdue."</li>
</ol>

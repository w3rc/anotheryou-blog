Recovery agents calling your relatives to shame you? RBI bans that

6 things a recovery agent is not allowed to do, and what to do when they do them anyway.

Why are we publishing this?

Because of comments like this one, under a video about loan recovery:

"Bhai mujhe to morning me 8 AM se hi Coll karke pareshan ho gaya Hu main"

Most people on the receiving end of those calls have never read the rules the callers are breaking. Here they are, in plain words. This doesn't cancel what you owe. It protects how you're treated while you sort it out.

The 6 things RBI says they must not do

RBI's rules for banks and for NBFCs (finance companies and the loan apps they run) say the lender and its agents must not:

  1. Intimidate or harass anyone, by words or physically.
  2. Humiliate you in public, or intrude on the privacy of your family members, referees and friends.
  3. Send inappropriate messages by phone or social media.
  4. Make threatening or anonymous calls.
  5. Call you again and again, or call before 8:00 am or after 7:00 pm to recover an overdue loan.
  6. Make false or misleading statements, like a fake legal notice or a threat of arrest.

"They call from 8 am. Is that allowed?"

An 8 am call is inside the window. Calling over and over isn't, and neither is a call at 7:30 am or 9 pm. Write down every call: date, time, number, what was said. Twenty calls before lunch looks a lot like the persistent calling the rules forbid, and your log is what proves it.

"They called my relatives and my friends"

Contacting people to humiliate you, or intruding on your family's and friends' privacy, is on the list above. Ask your relatives to screenshot the messages and note the call times. Those screenshots go straight into your complaint.

"Who is this person, and can I ask?"

You can. The lender has to tell you the recovery agency's name and contact details when your case is handed over, and an agent who visits has to carry the bank's authorisation letter and an identity card. Ask for both. Banks also have to list their recovery agencies on their website.

The bank is responsible for its agents

Under RBI's rules for banks, the bank answers for what its recovery agents do. So your complaint goes to the bank, not to the agent. And for banks there's one more rule: a case shouldn't be sent to recovery agents while your complaint about it is pending, unless the complaint is frivolous. Ask for the agency to be withdrawn from your account while it's being looked at.

"rbi complaint": the route, in order

  1. The bank, in writing. Write to the grievance redressal officer (named on the bank's website), name the agency, attach your call log and screenshots, quote the rule they broke, and ask for a reply. Keep the complaint number. A phone call alone is weak proof.
  2. Wait up to 30 days. If the bank rejects it, or doesn't reply in 30 days, you can go to the RBI Ombudsman.
  3. The RBI Ombudsman. File at cms.rbi.org.in within 90 days, counted from the bank's last reply or from the end of that 30 day wait, whichever is later. It's free, you don't need a lawyer, and on top of any money you actually lost it can award up to ₹3 lakh for loss of time, expenses, harassment and mental anguish.

When it goes past harassment

Threats of violence, extortion, or morphed photos of you are crimes: go to the police, and for anything online, 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in. If the "lender" is a loan app with no bank or NBFC behind it, it isn't RBI regulated at all; report it on cybercrime.gov.in and on RBI's sachet.rbi.org.in.
